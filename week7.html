Analysis of my learning for the week:

This week I focused mostly on the requirements my internship preparation program gave me. I managed to finish all of it so thankfully I’ll be officially in a micro internship soon. Other than that, I met with Khanh again this week. I listened to one of the conversations he was having with another student. Apparently one of the most notorious classes at Georgia Tech CS grad school is advanced algorithms. Go figure. I suppose algorithms are a hard topic for everyone. In order to help me feel more competent, I’ve continued to practice reading pseudocode and writing in one to help me focus on the fundamentals of data structure (as opposed to being bogged down by the minute details of a specific programming language’s quirk). Another topic we discussed is the idea of what a modern software engineer should focus on and be competent at: which is less so of being able to write code from scratch, and more so on being able to handle and catch up to a large codebase. This led to the discussion on the usage of AI. It seems the consensus is slowly pivoting towards the idea that using agentic AI is necessary for this skill. For this reason, I suppose the industry is focusing less on one’s ability to “freestyle” and write code from scratch. Instead, they’re focusing more on someone being able to understand the tech stack involved, the design architecture of the codebase, being able to immediately contribute to the codebase, etc. This does lead me to a question of: in that case, what exactly is the line that separates “professional” coding from “vibe” coding? Sometimes there are moments when I’m severely starting to question my ability when I start using AI. I feel that I understand why the AI wrote what it wrote, but do I actually? Or am I simply passively coasting along with what it wrote? Am I being able to practice “professional” and “best” design thoughtfully?
