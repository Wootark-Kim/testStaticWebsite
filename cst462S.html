CST 462S - Race, Gender, Class in the Digital World

"Provides students with key knowledge of race, gender, class and social justice especially in relation to technology in today’s digital world. Students challenge the barriers of expertise, gender, race, class, and location that restrict wider access to and understanding of the production and usage of new technologies. Students will engage in a practical experience in the community via their service placements, which will provide depth and context for considering questions of justice, equality, social responsibilities and the complexities of technology and its societal impact. The course uses scenario based approach combining presentations, discussions, and reflections to allow students explore the relationship between critical reflection and action on the topics mentioned above."

Learning Journal

The past 7 to 8 weeks, I've been fortunate to serve in a learning service program for the American Technology Initiative (ATI). The service actually started out quite south. During the first 2 weeks, communications weren't being aligned by both my teammates and the supervisor. Once the work started going, deliverables also started to pile as the project scope started to bloat.

On the other hand, such condition provided me with an opportunity to practice a great deal of not just technical work but also people and project management. By being the most responsive person, I unintentionally became the direct line of contact and served as a liaison on matching and aligning communication and project timeline between my teammates and the supervisor. I was made the GitLab admin for the webapp repository owned by ATI. I was able to set divided works amongst my teammates and follow up on their progress. I was able to lead by setting expectations and directing agendas in multiple team meetings between us and our site supervisor. I was able to resolve tensions in mis-alignment in expectations between the team and our supervisor. All of which, in retrospect, were incredible learning moments as these are skills that cannot be practiced through books or lectures alone. I would say this was the most impactful part during my service as I navigated through these challenges.

An advice I have for future Service Learning students is to be proactive, be communicative, and aim to push yourself without overstretching your limit. Any negative experiences you face may be an opportunity to practice a skill that can only be learned from real world interactions. Owned up to it and set a goal.
